# Arkham Horror: The Card Game Rulebook

## Overview

Arkham Horror: The Card Game is a cooperative game for 1 to 4 investigators. Each player controls an investigator with a personal deck, unique strengths, and weaknesses. Across a scenario or campaign, investigators gather clues, confront enemies, and survive the Mythos long enough to uncover the truth.

## Objective

In a scenario, investigators usually try to:

- explore locations
- discover clues
- advance the act deck
- survive enemies, treacheries, and the agenda deck

Advancing the act deck usually moves the investigators toward success. Advancing the agenda deck usually means the situation grows worse.

## Core Components

- investigator cards and mini investigator cards
- player decks
- act and agenda cards
- location cards
- enemy and treachery cards
- clue, doom, resource, damage, and horror tokens
- chaos bag

## Core Flow

Most turns revolve around:

1. moving between locations
2. investigating for clues
3. fighting or evading enemies
4. handling encounter pressure
5. stabilizing with cards and resources

## Round Sequence

Each round generally follows this order:

## 1. Mythos Phase

- place 1 doom on the current agenda
- if the doom threshold is met, advance the agenda
- each investigator draws and resolves 1 encounter card

## 2. Investigation Phase

Each investigator takes a turn.

During a turn, an investigator usually has **3 actions** to spend. Common actions include:

- move
- investigate
- fight
- evade
- play a card
- gain a resource
- draw a card

## 3. Enemy Phase

- enemies with `Hunter` move
- engaged enemies attack

## 4. Upkeep Phase

- reset actions
- ready exhausted cards
- each investigator draws 1 card and gains 1 resource
- each investigator checks hand size and discards down if needed

## Skills

Investigators use four core skills:

- Willpower
- Intellect
- Combat
- Agility

Examples:

- investigating usually uses Intellect
- fighting usually uses Combat
- evading usually uses Agility
- many encounter effects test Willpower

## Skill Tests

Skill tests are the heart of the game.

Basic procedure:

1. determine which skill is being tested
2. compare it to the test difficulty
3. commit cards or apply modifiers
4. reveal 1 chaos token from the chaos bag
5. apply the token modifier and any symbol effect
6. if the final value meets or exceeds the difficulty, the test succeeds

## Locations and Clues

Locations are where the story unfolds.

A location often has:

- a shroud value
- clues to discover
- text that changes how the location works

When you successfully investigate a location, you discover clues. Collecting enough clues often allows the investigators to advance the act deck.

## Enemies and Engagement

Enemies create pressure by:

- appearing at locations
- engaging investigators
- attacking during the enemy phase
- threatening attacks of opportunity

If an enemy is engaged with you, your choices become much narrower until you deal with it.

## Attacks of Opportunity

This is one of the most important rules for new players.

If you are engaged with a ready enemy and you take certain non-combat actions, that enemy may make an immediate attack of opportunity.

Common actions that can trigger one include:

- investigating
- drawing a card
- gaining a resource
- playing a card

Actions that usually do **not** trigger one include:

- fighting
- evading
- anything explicitly stated otherwise

## Fighting

To fight:

1. choose an enemy you can attack
2. perform a Combat skill test
3. if successful, deal damage to that enemy

Weapons often:

- increase Combat
- increase damage
- use ammo or other limited charges

## Evading

Evading buys time.

To evade:

1. choose an engaged enemy
2. perform an Agility test
3. if successful, that enemy exhausts and stops pressuring you for the moment

Evading is often better than fighting when your investigator is not built for damage.

## Damage and Horror

Investigators must track:

- damage
- horror

Too much damage defeats an investigator physically. Too much horror defeats them mentally.

Some allies and assets can absorb damage or horror on your behalf.

## Cards and Resources

Resources pay for cards. Cards are your flexibility and problem-solving tools.

Good play usually means balancing:

- board development
- enemy control
- clue gathering
- hand and resource economy

## Campaign Play

Multiple scenarios can be linked into a campaign.

Campaign play adds:

- story consequences
- experience gain
- deck upgrades
- trauma and persistent change

This is where the game’s long-form narrative becomes especially strong.

## New Player Advice

- Learn the round structure first
- Learn investigate, fight, evade, and skill tests before worrying about edge cases
- Always ask whether an engaged enemy will punish your next action
- Clues win scenarios, but enemy pressure cannot be ignored
- Clear team roles make the game easier: some investigators gather clues, others protect the group

## Common Mistakes

- forgetting doom in the mythos phase
- forgetting attacks of opportunity
- confusing passing an investigation test with actually discovering clues
- forgetting engaged enemies attack in the enemy phase
- overspending hand and resources too early
