# Startups Rulebook

## Objective

Startups is a fast majority card game about investing in companies and becoming the biggest shareholder at the right time. The player with the highest total value in capital chips at the end wins.

## Setup

- Shuffle the deck
- Remove 5 random cards from the game
- Deal 3 cards to each player
- Give each player 10 capital chips with the `1` side face up
- Place the remaining deck face down in the center

## Turn Structure

On your turn, do both of the following:

## 1. Take a Card

Choose one of these options:

### a. Draw from the deck

If you draw from the deck, place 1 capital chip with the `1` side face up on every card currently in the market.

If you cannot pay, you cannot choose this option.

### b. Take a card from the market

Take one card from the market together with all capital chips on it.

## 2. Play a Card

After taking a card, you must play one card:

### a. Into your portfolio

Place it face up in front of you.

### b. Into the market

Place it face up next to the deck.

If you took a company card from the market this turn, you cannot return a card from that same company to the market this turn.

## Anti-Monopoly Chips

Each company has an anti-monopoly chip. The player with the most shares in that company holds it.

### Tie Rule

If players are tied for the most shares, the player who received the chip earlier keeps it until another player exceeds them.

### While You Hold That Company’s Anti-Monopoly Chip

- Other players cannot take that company’s shares from the market
- When a player draws from the deck, they do not add a capital chip to that company’s shares in the market

## End of the Game

When the last card is drawn from the deck:

- that player finishes their turn
- then the game ends

After that, all players add the cards in their hand to their portfolio.

## Scoring

Resolve each company separately:

- the player with the most shares is the majority shareholder
- every other player who owns shares in that company pays the majority shareholder one `3`-value capital chip for each share they own in that company

### Ties for First

If two or more players are tied for the most shares:

- there is no unique majority shareholder
- nobody gains capital chips from that company

### If a Player Cannot Pay

If a player does not have enough capital chips:

- use plus and minus point markers
- do not pay using newly gained `3`-value chips from the same resolution

## Winning

- the player with the highest total capital value wins
- if tied, the player with more `3`-value chips wins
- if still tied, the player who played last wins

## Optional Variant: Rounds

In the rounds variant:

- after each round:
  - 1st place gains `+2`
  - 2nd place gains `+1`
  - last place gets `-1`
- restore the 5 removed cards and set up again
- the player who came last starts the next round
- after 4 rounds, the highest total score wins

If tied, compare:

1. number of `+2` chips
2. number of `+1` chips
3. winner of the last round

## New Player Advice

- Don’t spread yourself too thin across too many companies
- Market cards with capital on them can be very efficient, but they also reveal information
- Anti-monopoly chips are both protection and denial
- Winning is less about owning the most cards overall and more about controlling the right company at the right moment

## Common Mistakes

- counting cards in hand as current shares
- forgetting that ties for first mean nobody is paid
- taking a company from the market and then returning the same company to the market that turn
- overlooking the anti-monopoly restrictions
